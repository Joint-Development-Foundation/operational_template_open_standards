## Common Terminology and Conventions

This document is provided to help the Member or Contributor familiarize themselves with common terms, definitions, and concepts.

### Language
The default language for writing technical documentation and specifications is American English, English (United States). If your project is offering several translations of its specifications, it is standard practice to identify the English version as the canonical specification for conformance purposes.

### Conventions for Requirements
In IT specification writing, the IETF's [RFC2119](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2119) provides a well-established set of keywords (“MUST”, “MUST NOT”, “REQUIRED”, “SHALL”, “SHALL NOT”, “SHOULD”, “SHOULD NOT”, “RECOMMENDED”, “MAY”, and “OPTIONAL”) to indicate requirement levels in a specification. The JDF recommends following this convention for software and IT specifications.

Note that other standards developing organizations may use different conventions to indicate requirements. If you are planning to transpose your specification for advancement elsewhere, take a moment to review their conventions. Pay attention to any differences which may come to bear on the requirements set forth in your specification.
